## data

At the top level of the data model are songs. Songs have name, id, tempo, and a list of song-tracks. A song-track has a track id and a start (a start time).

A track has track-phases. A track-phase has speed, note-data-id, and a "follows-id". The follows-id refers to another track-phase-id under this track.

note-data block has elements with id, note (e.g. c4), tags, and bar-tag. the bar tag is the primary identifying tag. tags is a rich variety of data about how to play and or locate the note relatively.

these are stored in indexed db when it's available. if it is not available, they are stored in fake-indexeddb.

## runtime

the very core of the runtime is an rxjs observable that emits midi ticks. the rate of these ticks is determined by the tempo of the song that is loaded.

(temporary note: atm, all songs have a 120 tempo. variable tempo is a little down the road; but for now, the track-phase's speed can change the pace of emission.

the observable song emits its tempoized ticks. a track is a thin layer with a start time that does not mean very much at this early developmental stage. of more meaning are phases. the track is the direct owner of the phases.

the phases, which belong to a track, follow one another. when a song is first started, then, a midi-event becomes subscribable. the subscribed phases are powered by the ticks and begin to issue their note data.
